FORK is operations planning in the Business and Enterprise track. It helps a coordinator test a plan before a disruption makes the consequences real.

This synthetic food bank has two hundred forty meals, two hundred sixty four requested, and three delivery vehicles. The coordinator can compare plans before dispatch.

Three strategies use identical starting resources and different priorities. We show ordinary greedy routing too. It initially delivers two hundred twenty four meals, more than every named policy. The tradeoffs stay visible.

Now the same vehicle fails at the same time in every world. Completed deliveries stay completed, failed cargo stays stranded, and only the remaining plan can change.

With this breakdown, every adapted plan delivers one hundred sixty eight meals. Their fixed-plan controls deliver one hundred forty four, one hundred twenty, and one hundred forty four. Recovery is measured against the same shock, not an easier scenario.

A separate referee reconstructs movement, cargo, and timing, then recomputes the outcomes. This verifies consistency inside the declared model. It does not prove an optimal route or real-world safety.

A person reviews the evidence, approves the current revision, and exports the simulated plan. No vehicle is dispatched. FORK makes operational tradeoffs visible, with independent checks and a human making the decision.
